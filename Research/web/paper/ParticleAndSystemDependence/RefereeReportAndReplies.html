394 - How is the 13% skew determined? What is the skew?

We had a quick toy model to study the effects of limiting the range of the fit to exclude the peak with low statistics.  What we did was throw a random constant+Gaussian with a similar signal/background and comparable statistics to our signal for the bins where we had a residual track merging effect.  We then threw multiple samples in order to compare the true yield to the extracted yield. Of course with high statistics, the fits extract the true yield even when the peak is excluded from the fit, but the correlations with the residual track merging effect typically had much lower statistics than most correlations.  In this case we found that the mean yield extracted was skewed by about 13%, although it was usually within error of the true yield.  We note that for these data points the relative statistical error was typically 20-30% so when added in quadrature to 13%, the statistical error was still dominant.  We have expanded the discussion and included it in the discussion of the track merging section, in part to make it clear that this is only applied to the data where there is a track merging effect.
