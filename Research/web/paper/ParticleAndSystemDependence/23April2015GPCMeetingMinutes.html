    For all plots below The left red fit is Gaussian+constant fit to deta = 0.2, the blue fit is a Gaussian+ constant fit to deta = 0.3, and the black lines show the upper and lower bounds on the background extracted from each of these fits.  The left blue fit is a fit to a constant from deta = 1.25 to 1.75 and the right red fit is a fit to a constant from deta = 1.0 to 1.75.

    The standard method is to use the Gaussian fit with the fit either to deta = 0.2 or to 0.3, depending on the point and the width of the dip, to determine the background.  The black lines are therefore the range included in the default.

    There are two cases:
    1.  No evident dip:  The background determined from this Gaussian+const fit is used to determine the background level.  The statistical error on the background from the fit is added in quadrature to the statistical error due to bin counting.  I have added a sentence to the text of the paper because it was not previously explicitly stated in the paper text.
    "The statistical error on the yield from bin counting is added in quadrature to the statistical error on the background level from the fit."
    The error below on the bin counting method is now broken out into the statistical error due to the absolute value alone (first) and the statistical error due to the uncertainty on the background (second).
    2.  Dip:  The yield from the fit is used.  Whether it's from the left or right Gaussian fit depends on the size of the dip.

    This procedure is outlined in lines 283-280 of version 1.3.

    Below we put the default method in bold.  I found a typo in the errors on the bin counting method - this is fixed now.  They were too small.

    Comparing the impact of the background when the fit to const+Gaussian is used to when a constant alone is used is not straightforward.  We chose to use a parameterization of the Gaussian such that the parameter used was the yield, not the Gaussian amplitude - this means we avoid having to consider the covariance matrix of the fit because the parameter that is fit is the parameter we use in the analysis.  If we were to use information from other fits to calculate the background, we would have to consider the covariance matrix.

    While the background from the standard fit is not within error of all of the backgrounds, the yield is more uncertain with this fit because the fit isn't as good.  (We could instead use the fit with the narrower range but this would actually lead to a smaller uncertainty.)

    Note here that the error on the background is larger on the right (smaller range) but the uncertainty on the yield is smaller.  This is because the covariance between the yield and the background is non-trivial and it is therefore not straightforward to determine how to use the background from alternate methods when determining the yield when the fit is used to determine the yield.
