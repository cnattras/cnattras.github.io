It might therefore be useful to demonstrate that the results extracted here are ok. Any statements on change of dip with centrality and when do you give up? a large (12% sys error) has been attributed to this effect, how is it extracted?
In all the discussions, 'dip' is said to be due track merging, could it be due to some physics reason (exclusion of some sort).  
I remember in another star paper of correlation with identified particles (PA: Olga et al), corrections were made by looking at +-, -+ etc. I understand issues are more complicated due to decay products, but was there any thoughts on the issues?

No, there is no physics reason for the dip.  The dip was studied extensively by Marek and Leon, but no correction methods studied could reliable correct the dip for high pT triggered correlations. The track merging effect is well-documented and studied for HBT - but it has a strong pT dependence and it appears those methods cannot be extended to high pT for V0s in a straightforward way (although we did find that it worked well for h-h correlations).  We ultimately decided it was better to publish the data with a fit to correct for track merging rather than to not publish the data.   We did not attribute a 12% systematic error for the track merging effect but due to the fitting method.  We did a toy model study where we threw a distribution with a Gaussian plus a constant and we restricted the fit range to exclude the most central points, as we have to do when the dip due to track merging is present.  We found a skew when the center of the peak is not included for low-statistics data sets.  (All correlations where we use the fit to correct for track merging are also low statistics.)

2. What is the eta coverage? somewhere it is written 1.5, somewhere 1.0 and finally yield is <0.78? The TPC has an acceptance of |eta|<1.5 (line 189).  The tracks are restricted to |eta|<1.0. (line 197).  The yield we report is the integral over |delta-eta|<0.78 (equation 5).

3. In eff correction: embedding is done on pt, eta, centrality bins, why not phi as an axis? Any anisotropies in phi are taken care of by the mixed events, which correct for the impact of the acceptance on delta-phi and delta-eta (for instance due to the sector boundaries in the TPC).

Comments-I
==========
1)Range of yield determination |deta|<0.78. Whereas detector acceptance range |deta|=1.6./1.7 So it can be determined with a larger deta range so that some portion of the jet peak is not missed as there is a chance of Near side peak modification specially in case of Au-Au.. We see no evidence of any modification of the near-side peak on that scale.  The projections in deta are in the analysis note for every plot.  Including a wider range increase the statistical errors because a lot of numbers which are approximately 0 but have an error are included in the integral.

2)Why dphi projection is not used for yield determination. It is true that flow/bulk is flat in deta not in dphi. But one can fit the bulk and subtract it from the total correlation function. Even one can project the large deta(flow) part in dphi and use it to subtract the flow contribution and then determine yield from dphi projection itself.  Using dphi to determine the yield requires v2 and we think that using deta leads to a superior measurement because there are no systematic errors due to vn and the error bars due to the background level are smaller.  Using the dphi projections at large |deta| leads to comparable errors as well but is sensitive to whether or not there is any residual signal in the large |deta| region used for background subtraction.  We actually looked at these methods and opted for the deta method.

3)In case of Identified trigger correlation modification of the jet peak can be trigger species dependent. So using |deta ,0.78 can result in missing different amount of jet peak in different trigger species case. Has it been taken care properly..perhaps by a tentative peak shape(width) analysis... We define our yields to be the yield within |dphi|<0.78 and |deta|<0.78.

Comments-2

1. There is no clear indication in the abstract why this study is pursued ? We have rewritten the abstract in response to earlier comments.  We have explicitly added a motivation to the abstract.

2. It seems choice of trigger pT range 2<pT<6 GeV is done to address strange particle production at intermediate pT where recombination is likely to be dominant particle production mechanism. Similar analysis has already been done using non-strange particles to address the issue of "trigger dilution". Is the motivation of this study is conceptually

We looked at the composition of the jet-like correlation to see if we could see any evidence for recombination.  There are many possible mechanisms that could lead to different yields for identified triggers, including modification of fragmentation or different interactions between strange quarks and the medium.  Looking for these possible modifications was one of our motivations.

turn of hadronic rescattering in the final stage and look in to difference in correlated yield. Absence of hadron re-scattering will eliminate the mass dependent effects. We did not want to investigate rescattering or recombination in great detail and these data would probably not be precise enough to make a quantitative statement.  We opted to compare to PYTHIA because PYTHIA describes the jet-like correlation fairly well, however, our data are not precise enough to distinguish the differences between trigger particles even seen in PYTHIA.  Once the data are out, however, it would be great to see an independent model study.
