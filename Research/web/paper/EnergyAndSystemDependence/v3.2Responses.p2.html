Dear Colleagues,

Here are my comments to the "near-side dihadron" paper.

The main premise of this paper is that the same-side 2D peak
identified with jets in elementary collisions can be separated into a
"jet-like" component and a "ridge" component based on deviations from
a Gaussian ideal and the apparent eta width. It is concluded that the
"jet-like" component is a universal feature arising from in-vacuum
parton fragmentation described by PYTHIA while the "ridge" component
is something else, perhaps associated with hydro. There is no physical
justification (i.e., argument based on accepted physical theory such
as QCD) presented for separating the SS 2D peak into distinct parts.

As I have pointed out previously, all jets extend down to small hadron
momentum (< 0.5 GeV/c). Almost all hadron fragment momenta fall below
2 GeV/c, and almost all jet energies fall near 3 GeV. Typical
trigger-associated pt cuts therefore admit a small fraction of all
jets and a small fraction of *any* jet accepted. The result is an
artificial and highly-biased fragment population susceptible to
misinterpretation. That problem is exacerbated by ZYAM background
subtraction of 1D projections onto azimuth.


The pt cuts for this analysis are pt-trig in [3,6] GeV/c and pt-assoc
in [1.5,pt-trig] GeV/c. Why that choice? What would happen if those
numbers changed? In particular, how do these result compare to Estruct
minimum-bias studies as a limiting case? There is no reference to the
Estruct angular correlations paper recently submitted. Why not?

The choice of pt-trig and pt-assoc ranges was driven by our earlier work on PID triggered correlations in Au+Au collisions at 200 GeV and for other systems and energies also by available statistics at high-pT. The paper draft was one year in the GPC and at the time it was released by the GPC to the collaboration (August 2011) the arxiv reference to the Estruct correlation paper you are referring to did not exist. We have added the new reference and expanded a bit the sentence at the end of the first column on page 3.

General comment on figures: All plots in any paper should have tick
marks included on upper and right margins to match lower and left
margins.

We do not think it is necessary, it does not seem to be the rule in most of the STAR papers, neither it is required by the PRC journal style.

Fig. 14, Eq. (11) etc. -  Eq. (11) models a non-Gaussian SS 2D peak,
an AS 1D peak and a nonjet quadrupole in terms of a Gaussian 2D model
function and a single Fourier series. In Ref. [44] Sec. IV the
representation of periodic peak arrays is described in detail. SS and
AS peak arrays have *separate* Fourier series representations. The odd
multipoles are opposite in sign in the two cases. Combining the two
peaks into one Fourier series as in Eq. (11) misrepresents the 2D
system.

If the SS 2D peak is non-Gaussian, as with these pt cuts, some part of
the SS 2D peak (the "ridge") will be consigned in the fit erroneously
to the single Fourier series (a hoped for result). If the AS peak is
narrow the dipole approximation used for Estruct minimum-bias
correlations is no longer valid. The AS peak then contributes negative
odd terms to the single Fourier series depending on the AS peak width.
The *net* Fourier coefficients will then represent a confusing
combination of SS and AS peak characteristics. The result must be very
misleading.

L. 838 - The possibility is admitted that if the v3 term in the single
Fourier series is negative then that term could represent "nonflow"
(jets). But the possibility remains that jet structure dominates all
terms of the Fourier series in all cases.

L. 843 - "Positive values for v3 are consistent with expectations from
triangular initial conditions." That is the usual fallacious argument.
Positive values for v3 are also consistent with jet expectations, and
that is the more likely scenario.

Fig. 14 details - 	arXiv:1109.2540 provides details. See in particular
Fig. 11 (right panel) of that paper. That figure applies to
minimum-bias data where the AS peak is accurately described by a
dipole, thus no negative sextupole or v3 contribution appears. With pt
cuts the AS peak narrows and higher multipoles including the sextupole
become nonzero. The sign of the inferred v3 in Fig. 14 of this paper
then depends on the relative amplitudes of the *projected* SS 2D peak
and the AS 1D peak. For more-peripheral collisions the SS peak eta
width is small, so its 1D projection amplitude is small as well and
the AS negative sextupole wins. For more-central collisions the SS
peak eta width becomes large, as does its 1D projection amplitude. So
the SS peak positive sextupole wins. That explains the sign change
near Npart = 40 (sharp transition). The correspondent in
arXiv:1109.2540 is that the (SS) sextupole goes to zero below Npart ~
40 due to the eta exclusion cut specified in that panel (following the
Alver/Roland analysis). Again, the minimum-bias AS peak is pure dipole
and therefore contributes no negative sextupole component.

The v3/v2 ratio behavior for more-central collisions is also trivial.
In the numerator is the SS peak sextupole alone (jets). In the
denominator is the SS peak quadrupole (jets) plus the nonjet {2D}
quadrupole. The latter goes to zero in more-central collisions, so the
v3/v2 ratio goes asymptotically to a ratio of Fourier coefficients
times jet peak amplitudes. Hydro has nothing to do with that.

L. 898 - Again, the agreement of the "jet-like" part of the SS 2D peak
with PYTHIA should not be surprising. The arbitrary peak decomposition
is in effect designed to achieve that end. The part of the SS peak
near the angular origin corresponds to initial parton splittings at
larger energy scales which we should expect to be minimally modified
by environmental effects. Thus, PYTHIA should describe that part. The
part of the SS peak further from the angular origin corresponds to the
final splitting to hadrons at smaller energy scales which we should
expect to be maximally sensitive to environmental effects (e.g., color
fields).

By the assumptions of this analysis *any* deviations from ideal jet
formation are excised from jet physics and transferred to the flow
domain. We are in effect forbidden to study environmental modification
of fragmentation *which should be expected based on known jet
phenomenology*. Any such modifications are swept under the "ridge"
carpet, leading to confusion.

L. 918 - The two trends described here are both based on erroneous and
misleading methods: The result of ZYAM subtraction with biased v2 data
and the result of misrepresentation of a complex 2D correlation
structure by a single Fourier series and inappropriate 2D Gaussian
model function.

Any correlation analysis along the lines of this paper should present
primary ("raw," unsubtracted) correlation data based on a well-defined
numerical analysis *without assumptions about physics content*. Such
data should then be represented parametrically in tabular form in
terms of simple model functions that are demonstrated to be both
necessary and sufficient, and to employ the smallest number of
parameters possible. Correlation data so represented are then suitable
for model testing as an independent process. Any subsequent processing
of data based on physical models should be clearly identified as such,
and as therefore speculative.

In contrast, the present analysis *from the beginning* parses
correlation structure based on strong (and unjustified) physical
assumptions and continues throughout to bias data processing and
interpretation in favor of a particular theoretical context: in-vacuum
jets coexist with a "ridge" conjectured to arise from some hydro
process. The more reasonable hypothesis, that jet shapes are strongly
altered by the environment in A-A collisions, is not even mentioned as
a possibility.
