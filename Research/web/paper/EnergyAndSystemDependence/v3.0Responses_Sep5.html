8) Statement from lines 624-625, ‘dominantly produced by vacuum fragmentation’. This conclusion comes from the fact that the yield in the jet component does not change much from d+Au, Cu+Cu and Au+Au collisions. In the late part of the paper there seems to be some difference in Delta-eta and Delta-phi width associated with these collisions. So we probably should not make such a strong statement about vaccum fragmentation. It is possible that the presence of bulk partonic matter just does not significantly alter the yield in the measured pT region associated with jets while changing the phi and eta angles somewhat.
The full phrase is, "...as expected if the jet-like correlation were dominantly produced by vacuum fragmentation."  This does not indicate that the only possibly production mechanism is vacuum fragmentation, just that it is consistent with that expectation.  We therefore feel that this is not an excessively strong statement.

10) In Figure 12, the data for Au+Au at 62 GeV are negative. The statistical error is not very large for these points. That is a systematic issue. It may be useful to constrain the minimum yield to be above zero.

IV: Questions: 1. Line 631, Could you please explain the physics reasons why the PYTHIA prediction is above data? Not just say "PYTHIA simulation is for p+pcollisions".
The short answer is that PYTHIA was not tuned to these data.  Different PYTHIA tunes either overshoot or undershoot.  For comparison to these data we chose the tune that had been most carefully tuned to similar measurements, di-hadron correlations done by CDF.  The CDF data are in a slightly higher kinematic region.  This discrepancy between PYTHIA Tune A and the data is much smaller than the difference between different tunes of PYTHIA.   If the mechanisms for particle production in PYTHIA at these momenta (dominantly multiparton scattering) are correct, this really just means they need to tune their Monte Carlo to the data. There is no sustantial physical reason behind the difference.  We therefore do not think that it is worth elaborating on in the text.

Page 8, Fig 5 - the data presented here show no justification of using the dEta range outlined by the vertical lines for the jet signal extraction. Page 9, lines 562-566 -- "A narrow ... Gaussian away-side peak... would lead to a negative v3..." - this statement is subject to many conditions (the width of the gauss, relative (same-away) peak strength, presence of the triangular flow, etc.) . CMS studies have shown, for example, that in the similar kinematic track cuts, the away-side in peripheral events leads to negative v1, not v3. Please tone down to "COULD lead to negative v3"
In one of the exercises done during the GPC, we investigated the v3^2 of a Gaussian by calculating the Fourier component.  This exercise is discussed here.  The end result was that v3^2>0 for a Gaussian with a width less than 1.5 units in azimuth.  This is why we added the word "narrow." A narrow Gaussian does have a negative v3^2.  The sign of the contribution from a Gaussian away-side is not impacted by either its amplitude or the amplitude of the near-side.  We have refined our wording to state that this would be a negative contribution to v3-tilde^2 and if v3-tilde^2 is negative overall, this could indicate that non-flow is dominant.  Helen & Joern?
Page 9, Figure 7 -- is a major cause for our concern with the analysis - at least half the plots shown have extended negative-signal ranges, contradicting to the assumptions stated earlier. What is the reason for this "over-subtraction"? How is this accounted for in the signal extraction and systematic errors?
The background subtraction is determined by a fit to the data.  There is a statistical error on the background subtraction, included in the statistical error bars on the yields.  Given that this is statistical, about half of the time the background will be too high and about half of the time it will be too low.  The yield is determined by bin counting and the error on the background is propagated for every data point.  Helen & Joern
