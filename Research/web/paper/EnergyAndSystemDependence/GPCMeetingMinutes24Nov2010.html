1. Is the explanation of the track merging correction clear?

    Lijuan thinks it is quite clearly explained in the paper. Fuqiang asked whether we did exactly the same correction as Marco/Mark in their di-hadron paper (simple reflection of data points around dphi=0). Christine explained that our correction is more refined, because we are using different pt-regime than Marco/Mark's paper, namely higher-pT where the tracks are closer to each other and thus true merging at dphi-0 plays a larger role in our analysis. In addition the more precise method used in our draft is motivated by its possible applicability to correlations with V0-particles, which are even more sensitive to track merging correction than di-hadron correlations.

   Further we discussed Jana's suggestion whether it would be perhaps good to include "a cartoon" showing track crossing/merging. The options are either dirrectly in Fig. 3 or to create a new figure with the cartoon only. There was a general agreement, that this could help in clarity of the paper for a non-expert reader. PA's will prepare the cartoon and circulate it in the GPC mailing list by next meeting.

   Lijuan asked what exactly means that at large dphi and deta we apply "fast mixing" correction. Christine and Jana explained that this correction is based on having from the real data stored histograms of phi and eta acceptance of charged trigger and associated tracks sampled in centrality and pt bins from which are then randomly generated pairs of trigger-associated particles and derived the TPC sector boundary correction in dphi as well as the triangular correction in deta space at dphi and deta > dip due to merging/crossing of tracks. Both corrections are then smoothly combined in the overlap area. The advantage of this "fast mixing" technique is that it is very fast contrary to "regular mixing" which requires a lot of CPU.

2. Raw correlation plots at the paper webpage

    At the last meeting, Paul raised a question of posting the raw correlation data (i.e. before ZYAM subtraction) on the paper webpage once the paper is published to enable physics community e.g. to apply other methods of background subtraction. We discussed in a great detail what the options would be as there are in order of 100 correlation plots which need to be posted on the web (eps figures, root macro/root file with histograms, ascii tables). We concluded that the format should be portable enough to allow a non-STAR person to understand it and use it. Lijuan suggested to ask Bedanga, whether root files with data points would be a sufficient compromise.


3. Systematic errors of v2 in CuCu vs AuAu 200 GeV

 Christine said that Helen and Paul talked in person at the STAR collaboration meeting about v2 syst. errors (unfortunately neither Christine, nor Jana were at the meeting). This issue has been already raised at the last GPC meeting, as currently the v2 syst. errors of CuCu 200 GeV data are much smaller than those of AuAu 200 GeV data, despite the fact that naively one would expect opposite behaviour due to larger non-flow contributions in CuCu. The outcome of the discussion of Helen and Paul was following (a shortened version of Helen's summary of their discussion):

  There has been apparently an attempt to measure v2{4} in the CuCu but the results are not under control and are unlikely to come out so there is no way to make the same sys. study for CuCu as for AuAu. Paul and Helen agreed that one could therefore reduce down the AuAu error by estimating it from the same set of studies that have been made in CuCu to make a) a fair comparison of the correlation results between the two systems and b) we also don't really know which is the best set of v2 measurements to contrast to decide what the error on the correlation measures is. So as long as one describes exactly what v2 measurements are used to estimate the error and how its obtained and one "publishes"/makes available to the world the unsubtracted correlations and the N_trig values one can reverse engineer "new" results if/when a better understanding of the background subtraction becomes available. Would that be acceptable by collaboration as clearly a different v2 syst. would be used than that already officially published ...

  We decided to finalize this discussion during the next GPC meeting as unfortunately Paul had to leave before we came to this topic.
