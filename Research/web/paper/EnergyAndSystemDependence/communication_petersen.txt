March 1, 2011 (continued):

Dear Jana and Christine,

we have discussed the results and did not find any 'trivial' factor of 2 because of different definitions or so. So, here is exactly what we have calculated:

For 1/N_trig dN/d\delta\phi distribution, the Fourier coefficients is the ratio of 1/N_evt * \sum_evt {1/N_trigger \sum_pair cos[n(\phi_i-\phi_j)]}_evt and 1/N_evt * \sum_evt {1/N_trigger \sum_pair}_evt (since the distribution is not normalized to 1). The numerator is: for a given event sum over cos[n(\phi_i-\phi_j)] for all pairs and then average over the trigger particle number, and then take the event average, the denominator is just the event average of associated particle number.
As particle pairs we have taken exactly the trigger/associated pt combinations as you indicated. We have included all (also neutral) particles within |eta|<1.
For the ratio of the Fourier coefficients the denominators cancel, so we actually do not need them. We have checked that for 'untriggered' two-particle correlations (where both particles have pt<3 GeV) the result from this calculation correspond to the integrated v2/v3 values that we have published using the event plane method. For high pt this comparison cannot simply be done, since the high pt results are very sensitive to auto-correlations and the weight in the event plane definition and so on.

Coming back to the comparison of your results to our calculation: Since we have only collective behaviour from hydrodynamics and also our high pt particles come from the high pt tails of the local thermal distributions our values for v3 and v2 are larger and so is the relative value of the squares of the Fourier coefficients.
In the data, your high pt particles have most likely a large contribution from jets, so the collective response is smaller.
That is our take on this so far.
With best wishes,
Hannah
