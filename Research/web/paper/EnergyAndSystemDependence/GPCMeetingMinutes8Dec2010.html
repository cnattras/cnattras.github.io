1. Christine showed an update on 2D fits (pls see the new version of analysis note: page 50-53). The resulting ridge yields are generally higher than in the case of ZYAM bkg. subtraction method, but the trend, namely the ridge increase with centrality is the same and therefore the physics conclusion drawn in the paper based on the ZYAM bkg. subtraction are solid. Further we discussed whether the 2D analysis the way it is done is sufficient to satisfy Lanny's question regarding the v2(2D). The general consensus is that it is and we won't use the v2(2D) for the v2 estimate in order to be consistent with earlier correlation papers. It was noted that Christine sent the 2D fits to Lanny Nov. 22 and did not receive a response yet.


2. v3 values (see table on page 52 in the Analysis note): Paul commented that the v3 extracted values are large. The source of this was identified as PA's used a different definition of  v3. The 3rd Fourier component shown in the table (p. 52), was extracted from the equation F (p. 50) and thus corresponds to "v3"=B*v3^2. PA's will update the table with the proper v3 values. (already done) Paul pointed out that Teaney (http://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.1876) shows that v3 is strongly pT dependent (Fig. 12) and the estimated v3 contribution in the STAR data in the event plane correlation paper could be therefore underestimated. This should be acknowledged in the energy/system paper draft.


3. Hua asked about the cos(dphi) momentum conservation term and its origin and we discussed how one could understand it and how this compares to the estruct analysis.

4. Christine presented the new version of Figure 12 (ridge yield vs Npart) now including also the momentum kick model predictions from Wong as Manuel requested. The main message of the momentum kick model calculation is that the ridge yield is smaller at 62 GeV than at 200 GeV (in line with data), but at given energy it is always predicted to be larger in Cu+Cu than in Au+Au collisions (while within errors, Cu+Cu and Au+Au data are consistent). Christine brought up that the momentum kick model may have its ows "systematic errors" and we should contact Wong before we release the paper in order to know e.g. how significant is the suggested difference between collision systems at a given energy. Paul brought up that many theorists do not believe that the momentum kick model is correct as it violates causality.

The PA's are aware that the new figure after including the model lines looks too busy. We discussed possible solutions how to make the figure better legible:
a) split the figure into two panels, showing separately the two collision energies or systems. This would certainly help the legibility of the figure as such, but the PA's would prefer to keep the data all in one plot.
b) show the v2 systematic error bars as "bars or brackets" instead of the error bands.
c) discuss the momentum kick model predictions only in the text without putting the lines explicitly to Figure 12.

PA's tend toward the third option. Manuel, would you be happy with this solution?
